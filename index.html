About Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)

What is AMR?

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) occurs when bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites no longer respond to antimicrobial medicines. As a result, infections can become more difficult to treat, increasing the risk of disease spread, severe illness and death.

Everyone has a role in preventing AMR. Use antibiotics only when prescribed by a qualified healthcare professional, follow medical advice, maintain good hygiene and help prevent infections.

AMR Awareness Pledge

I pledge to act responsibly and contribute to preventing antimicrobial resistance.

Pledges for Adults and Families

    For minor illnesses:

  • Use antibiotics and other antimicrobials only when prescribed by a qualified healthcare professional.
  • Don't ask for antibiotics for coughs, colds, sore throats, or flu, since these are viral infections..

    • For prescription adherence:

  • Always take antibiotics exactly as prescribed, never skip doses, and never save them for future use.

    • For self-care:

  • Talk to a pharmacist first for advice on treating cold and flu symptoms without a doctor's prescription.

    • For hygiene:

  • Wash hands regularly with soap and water to prevent the spread of infections.

Pledges for Pet Owners

    For veterinary care:

  • Follow your vet's advice exactly and do not demand antibiotics if they state they are not necessary for your pet.

    • For administration:

  • Never give human medicines or leftover antibiotics to your pets.

Pledges for Prescribers (Doctors, Consultants, & GPs)

    For diagnostics:

  • Use local prescribing guidelines and diagnostic tools (like CRP testing) to confirm infection types before ordering antibiotics.

    • For delayed prescriptions:

  • Use delayed or post-dated prescriptions where appropriate for self-limiting conditions.

    • For review:

  • Review antibiotic prescriptions after 48 to 72 hours to step down, switch to oral, or stop treatment completely.

Pledges for Pharmacists & Pharmacy Teams

    For patient education:

  • Talk to patients about the normal duration of common self-limiting infections (like a cough lasting up to 3 weeks).

    • For symptom management:

  • Recommend evidence-based over-the-counter remedies to treat symptoms instead of antibiotics.

    • For disposal:

  • Encourage patients to return unused antibiotics to the pharmacy for safe disposal rather than throwing them in the bin.

Pledges for Nurses, Midwives, & Allied Health

    For infection control:

  • Strictly adhere to hand hygiene and standard infection prevention protocols to stop the spread of resistant bugs.

    • For communication:

  • Educate patients and their families on why antibiotics do not work against viral infections like colds and flu..

Pledges for Veterinary Professionals

    For animal health:

  • Follow the highest standards of veterinary stewardship by using narrow-spectrum antibiotics as a first-line treatment only when necessary.

Together, we can preserve the effectiveness of antimicrobials for future generations.
